Core Pillar Comparisons

Key dimensions analyzed through opposing historical lenses

Colonial Account

Spanish Perspective

Jose Montero y Vidal
NATURE OF THE EVENT

Premeditated Grand Rebellion

Described not merely as a labor dispute, but as part of a widespread, orchestrated conspiracy to completely overthrow Spanish sovereignty in the Philippine islands and massacre Spanish officers.

Portrayed as a treasonous national revolt backed by native clergy and lawyers to establish an independent government.
PRIMARY ROOT CAUSE

Anti-Spanish Conspiracy & Instigation

Blamed on democratic ideas arriving from Europe, unrest stoked by native secular priests (*clerigos*), educated elites (*ilustrados*), and propaganda aiming to sever ties with the Spanish Crown.

  • Removal of worker tax exemptions was downplayed as a minor trigger.
  • Instigators allegedly used promises of wealth and rank to lure soldiers.
ROLE OF GOMBURZA

Conspirators & Masterminds

Fathers Gomez, Burgos, and Zamora were identified as the active orchestrators and financial backers who incited troops with revolutionary sentiment.

Official Verdict: Found guilty of treason and sedition, sentenced to execution by garrote to crush future dissent.
Reformist Account

Filipino Perspective

Pardo de Tavera / Plauchut
NATURE OF THE EVENT

Localized Mutiny & Arsenal Strike

Characterized as a minor, isolated labor uprising strictly limited to the soldiers and arsenal workers of Fort San Felipe under Sergeant Lamadrid.

A spontaneous protest caused by worker grievances, exaggerated by Spanish authorities into a national rebellion to retain power.
PRIMARY ROOT CAUSE

Abolition of Worker Privileges

Directly triggered by Governor-General Rafael Izquierdo’s harsh decree removing longstanding exemptions from paying tribute tax and performing forced labor (*polo y servicios*).

  • Workers were outraged by sudden pay deductions and forced labor duties.
  • No overarching plan existed to capture Manila or install a new government.
ROLE OF GOMBURZA

Innocent Scapegoats & Martyrs

The priests had no direct connection to the arsenal riot. Spanish friars used the mutiny as a convenient pretext to execute secularization leaders.

Historical Outcome: Their public execution awakened Filipino national consciousness, directly inspiring Dr. Jose Rizal and the Propaganda Movement.

Key Chroniclers & Figures

Primary sources and historical actors behind each narrative

JM

Jose Montero y Vidal

Spanish Historian

Wrote the official Spanish historiographical account in *Historia General de Filipinas*, emphasizing a widespread conspiracy.

RI

Gov. Rafael Izquierdo

Governor-General

Abolished worker privileges and submitted official reports to Madrid framing the mutiny as an anti-Spanish revolt.

PT

Dr. T.H. Pardo de Tavera

Filipino Scholar

Authored the Filipino version showing the mutiny was purely a response to oppressive labor and tax policies.

G

GOMBURZA

Secular Clergy Martyrs

Fathers Gomez, Burgos, and Zamora. Executed on Feb 17, 1872; became catalyst for Philippine nationalism.
